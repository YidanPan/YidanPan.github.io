The Magic of Showing Up–Mere Exposure Effect

Hello, everyone. Today, my topic of speech is the mere exposure effect, which shows the magic of showing up.

Now, think about that, have you ever hated a pop song first, but when you hear it twenty times, you found yourself humming along. This is the Mere Exposure Effect in action. It’s a psychological phenomenon where people develop a preference for things merely because they are familiar with.

So here, I want to talk about it by three key words.

First, Frequency. This is the starting point. In the business world, merchants are playing a game of repetition with you, they bet that when you see their brands many times, they can win the top-of-mind position before you even realize it. So they spend millions on ads not just to sell, but to increase the frequency of interaction.

This leads to our second keyword, Familiarity. It’s the direct result of frequency. The evolutionary theory teaches us that familiar equals safe. So when faced with too many choices, we will choose the most familiar one. That’s why many famous brands like Coca-Cola and Tiktok never stop advertising. They want to be a familiar face in your daily life.

Finally, the ultimate core of this logic is Fluency. It is the key driving force behind our decision. Because of the familiarity, our brain will process the information much faster and easier. As we all know that our brain is lazy, it just like to memorize something easy, so they often confuse ‘easy to process’ with ‘high quality’. That’s why we trust the brand we are familiar with even though their quality is not so good.

In conclusion, the Mere Exposure Effect creates a powerful “exposure trap.” So, the next time you reach for a famous brand, ask yourself: “Do I really like this product, or is my brain just playing a game of familiarity with me?”

That’s all, thanks for your listening.
